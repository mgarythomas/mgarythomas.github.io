## Introduction

"How much should we let the artificial intelligence (AI) decide?" is the wrong question, and it is the one every governance committee opens with.

It is wrong because it collapses two independent variables into one, and the collapse is where bad autonomy policy comes from.

## Methodology

Axis one — what the system earned. Demonstrated, measured capability, graduated in levels. Earned through evidence over time, never granted by procurement, and revocable when performance drops.

Axis two — what the decision permits. A property of the decision, not the system. Consequence, reversibility, blast radius, regulatory exposure.

Hold them apart and the policy writes itself:

Autonomy cap = min(earned level, permitted level).

A system that earned the top of the ladder still drops to advisory for a catastrophic, irreversible call — not because it is untrustworthy, but because that is what "irreversible" means. And a system that earned very little stays supervised even on trivial decisions, until it earns more.

The practical failure is almost always a single global setting. One autonomy level, chosen once, applied to everything — which means it is simultaneously too permissive for the decisions that matter and too restrictive for the ninety percent that do not, so people route around it. A control everyone bypasses is not a control.

Grade the decisions. Grade the system. Take the minimum. Write it down.
